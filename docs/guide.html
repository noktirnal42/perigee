Guide

Fix stalls, step by step

A workflow for cloud gaming (Xbox Cloud Gaming, GeForce NOW, Steam Link) over Starlink. The rule: change one thing, then measure again.

Before you start. Join your Starlink Wi-Fi, open Perigee, and check Diagnose → What this connection allows. Anything marked locked is blocked by the router itself, not by Perigee.

1 · Measure the hop that matters

Open Diagnose → Stall tester. Pick Router as the target (this isolates the Wi-Fi hop between your device and the router), label the run “baseline”, and press Start 30 s test.

  • Loss > 1% or stalls ≥ 3 = a real problem for cloud gaming.
  • A stall is any ping that's lost or > 20 ms; samples within 1 s merge into one episode. “Every 3 s” is a classic interference signature.
  • Run Dish too: if the dish is clean but the router isn't, the problem is your Wi-Fi, not the satellite link.

Then open Network → Radios. A 5 GHz frame-error rate above ~2% means the radio is receiving corrupted frames — interference.

2 · Pick a stable channel; avoid DFS

By default the router chooses its 5 GHz channel and may move it. In Tune → 5 GHz → Channel, pin one. Prefer non-DFS channels (36–48 or 149–161): on DFS channels (52–144) the router must evacuate instantly when it hears radar, which drops every device.

  • Turn DFS off in Radio behaviour for the steadiest link.
  • If one block is noisy, try the other: 36–48 ↔ 149–161.

3 · Use 80 MHz, not 160

Wider channels carry more peak throughput but need a clean slice of spectrum. For latency-sensitive play, 80 MHz is the sweet spot. Leave wireless mode on automatic unless an old device misbehaves.

4 · Tame bufferbloat with CAKE

Bufferbloat is the lag that appears when something else saturates your link. CAKE is a smart queue that keeps latency flat by shaping slightly under your real line speed.

  1. Run a speed test and note your download rate.
  2. In Tune → Smart queue (CAKE), set the rate to about 90% of it, choose Diffserv 4 so games outrank bulk downloads, and apply.
  3. Re-test the stall tester while a large download runs.
Units caveat. The router's schema doesn't state the unit of the CAKE bandwidth field. Perigee assumes Mbit/s (the router's own ACK-queue values suggest it). Verify with a speed test after applying; revert is one tap.

5 · A/B test your change

After each change run the stall tester again with a new label (“ch149 80MHz”). In Saved runs, tick two or more and press Compare for side-by-side bars of stalls, loss and p95, plus an overlaid latency trace. Perigee records the router's settings with each run so you can always see why two runs differ.

6 · Reverting

Every write shows a Revert button in the result and in Changes this session. It writes the previous value back as another verified single-field change. (A setting that was previously “not set” can't be written back exactly, so Perigee tells you instead of guessing.)

Troubleshooting

SymptomWhat to try
“Not on your Starlink network”Join the router's Wi-Fi or Ethernet. On iOS allow Local Network for Perigee in Settings › Privacy.
Everything works but “Channel survey” is lockedExpected: the router refuses it over the LAN. Perigee uses airtime counters instead.
Wi-Fi dropped after applyingNormal: a radio change restarts Wi-Fi for 10–40 s. Rejoin; Perigee finishes verifying automatically.
Writes are greyed outTurn on Enable writes at the top of the Tune tab.
No satellite matchSet your location in Satellites. The dish's GPS isn't readable from the LAN.
